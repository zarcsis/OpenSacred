# Drawing the world: the order of the objects

Recovered from the GOG `Sacred.exe` 2.28 with Ghidra 12.1.4 on 2026-10-18.
The draw lists and the cells' chains are in [docs/02 §6.1](../02-data-formats.md#61-itemspak-itm-v5-the-object-template-database) and [§5.5](../02-data-formats.md#55-staticpak-floorpak-triggerspak); the depth formula is in [§5.3](../02-data-formats.md#53-heights-and-the-render-contract).
Status: **confirmed** on the code unless marked otherwise.
Implemented in `opensacred-game` (`statics`, `backpack`, `fade_points`, `models`, `blended`, `buildings`, `shadows`, `shadow_dots`, `fx`, `model_effects`, `choice`, `skins`, `terrain`, `iso`, `srgb`, `sprites.wesl`, `models.wesl`, `order.wesl`, `standard.wesl`, `prepass.wesl`, `nearest.wesl`, `shadows.wesl`), `opensacred-rules` (`spells`), `opensacred-convert` (`world`, `model_scales`), `opensacred-data` (`world`, `tables`) and `opensacred-formats` (`world`, `items`).

## The rows

A frame's world pass (the function around `0x632100`…`0x6328B0`) walks the screen's rows of diamonds from the top, two rows a step (the cell index advances by `0x41`, one cell along each axis, between the step's two rows), and hands each row to `0x62AE90`.
A row is a diagonal of the grid, `cx + cy` constant.
`0x62AE90` walks it from left to right: from a cell to the next the index moves by `−0x3F`, one column on and one row back, so `cx` grows by 1 and `cy` falls by 1, and the walk crosses into the neighbouring sector of the 3 × 3 that are loaded when it leaves one.

In each cell `0x62AE90` draws the ground's quad, then walks the cell's chain of statics (the head at `+0x04` of the cell, the next at `+0x1F` of each record) and appends each drawn object to a draw list.
The lists are vectors of 24-byte entries (a kind, the object's index, its screen position as floats and as integers), and every entry is appended at the end, so a list holds its objects in the order of the walk: by row, then left to right, then in the order of the cell's chain.
The kinds seen: 1 a sprite, 2 a miniature (object flag `0x20`), 8 an entry of the list read as list 1 (docs/02 §6.1), `0x11` a sprite of a type with bit `0x10000` when `DETAILLEVEL` is 1 or more, its mask is 2 or more and it lacks flag `0x800`.

After the last row the same function draws, in order (read 2026-10-18): the ground's transitions (`0x62D3C0`), the objects of the liquid cells (`0x62E410`, below, when there are any), the liquids' surfaces (`0x62DD00`, docs/re/liquids.md), and the five lists (`0x62FF60`), each list in turn and its entries in their order.
So every object of an earlier list lies under every object of a later list, whatever their rows.
`0x62FF60` draws a miniature where it stands among the list's sprites, as it does a sprite, without the depth test.
Before its first list it draws the creatures' footprints ("The footprints" below), so they lie over the ground and the liquids' surfaces and under every object of the lists.

## The liquid cells

In a cell whose nature is a liquid (`+0x1F & 0xF0` of `0x90`, `WATERA`, or `0xA0`, `WATERB`, docs/02 §5.2), `0x62AE90` appends the miniatures that would go to list 0 (of a type with bit `0x4`) to a vector of their own instead (`0xAD7848`, of the lists' 24-byte entries), and with them the world's objects of the cell whose type passes `0x434070`, that lack flag `0x100000` at `+0x14`, whose virtual function at `+0x24` answers 0 to `0x2A`, and whose type is not a chest's (category 4, `0x428440`).
`0x434070` passes a type of the category `0x20` (`items.pak +0x2E`), the waters' crab and fish 163 to 166 (docs/re/ai.md), and a type whose `Weapon.pak` index (`+0x18`, docs/02 §6.1) is above 0 and below the count at `+0x563000` of the items table, an item (read 2026-10-25, #983, the call at `0x62CB0E`); an FX object never goes there, as the walk takes it in a pass of its own ("The models" below, #2941).
The flag `0x100000` is the flight's (`0x44DED0`; docs/re/arts.md), so an object in the air, a thrown drop or a creature thrown, is never sent there.
The query `0x2A` (the object's virtual `+0x24`, asked nowhere else) answers 0 for every object but an arrow: `cArrow3D`'s query (`0x5BAFF0`, its virtual table `0x891AB4`) answers 1 while its `+0x1E4` bit 1 holds, and only to `0x2A`; `cObject3D`'s (`0x450930`), `cCreature`'s (`0x55D260`), `cItemBase`'s (`0x5C70C0`) and those of its classes that pass the rest on to it (the weapons' `0x5CED50`, the chests' `0x5CA430`, the potions' `0x5CBAD0`) have no case of it (read 2026-11-20, #3065, the queries of every virtual table of the objects' base).
`0x62E410` draws that vector as `0x62FF60` draws a list, entry by entry with the same code for each kind, before the liquids' surfaces: those objects lie under the water.
So the surface, as opaque as the bed is deep (docs/re/liquids.md), and the clear waters' second layer, added to the frame, lie over a fish, its shadow, which its draw makes right before it ("The models' shadows" below), and an item lying in the water.
**Seen** 2026-10-25 with #448's driver: in 50,39's pond, over a bed of −10, the waters' shoals and three more made with `spawn` showed as pale, water-tinted shapes under the surface, the 164's leaping fish too, in an average of 16 frames against 16 without them; in a single frame they are faint slivers that the water's animation nearly hides.
The material is the byte `+0xF7` (`WATERA`) or `+0xF8` (`WATERB`) of the sector's record of docs/re/daynight.md, at that record's place keyx `+0x2E0` and `+0x2E1`, the sector's two liquid slots (docs/02 §5.1).
Over the world 12,127 miniatures lie in liquid cells (12,024 of `WATERA`, 103 of `WATERB`), counted on the sector files 2026-10-18.

## The footprints

Read 2026-11-21 for #3066 in the code alone, the original not run: a creature's step leaves a print on sand and on snow and a ripple on the two waters, a flat picture kept in one of 64 slots of the world and drawn at the head of the lists' walk.
The addresses and the numbers below are the code's; what they make on the screen is a **hypothesis** until frames of the running original bear it out ("What frames must settle" at the section's end), and what the data shows is marked **confirmed**.

**The step.**
`0x639080(creature's id, its place, its heading, the foot's point)` is called by the creature's animation step (`0x5941A0`, at `0x5984BB` to `0x598740`) at each of the walk's step moments, the two feet's (the bones `Bip01 R Foot` and `Bip01 L Foot`, 36 and 37 of the names' table `0x8E7200`) and, for a model that has them, the two hands' (2 and 1), as docs/re/sound.md, "The other creatures' footsteps", reads them.
- The place is the creature's own (`+0x18`: the sector, `x` and `y` in world units, the floor), not the bone's: the bone's point is used only by the fire of the set 23 and of the Big Demon (docs/re/sound.md, "Footsteps by the ground").
- The heading is the creature's direction (`+0x70`) as an angle on the screen (`0x450680`): the direction through the cells' projection, then `atan2(−y, x)` in degrees, 0° to the right and 90° up.
- A creature in the air (`+0x1F4` bit `0x200000`) and the Daemon in her flying form (`0x691260`: the sound manager's record of the type 9 while its `+0xAF49` is set) never reach the step: their moments play the wings' sound alone.
- Each call lays at most one picture, so a walker lays one at each foot's moment and a four-legged model one at each of its four.

**Who lays one.**
- The Big Demon (the type 53) and a player's hero of `+0x1FC` bit 1 make their fire 878 and no footprint (`0x63913D`, `0x63918C`).
- After the footstep's sound the step asks the type (`0x426480`, at `0x6397E9`): a type of the `Creature.pak` class 4, the horses, lays one; any other lays none where its `items.pak` flags (`+0x00`) hold bit `0x4000`, or where its `Creature.pak` class is 11, and one otherwise.
- **Confirmed** on the tables: bit `0x4000` is bit `0x40` of the record's class byte, set in 73 creature types, all named `TYPE_NATURE_` (512 to 600, the animals), nine of them the horses of the class 4; the class 11, the ghosts' (docs/re/fight.md, "The archers"), has 12 types.
  So of the 473 creature types of `Creature.pak` 397 pass, the Big Demon among them, which makes its fire instead, and the 64 animals that are no horses and the 12 of the class 11 lay none.
- Nothing asks whose creature it is, how far it is or whether it is on the screen: every creature whose animation step runs lays them, the hero as any other.

**What it lays**, by the nature of the cell under the creature (`+0x1F & 0xF0`; the switch at `0x6392AE`, byte table `0x639AB0`, jump table `0x639A9C`):

| Nature | The picture |
|---|---|
| `DESERT` (`0x10`) | `FOOTSTEPDESERT.TGA`, one print |
| `ARCTIC` (`0x20`) | `FOOTSTEPSNOW.TGA`, one print |
| `SUMPF` (`0x50`) | `FOOTSTEPSWAMP.TGA`, one print |
| `WATERA` (`0x90`), `WATERB` (`0xA0`) | `footstepWaterA_01.tga` to `05`, a ripple of five frames |
| any other | none |

- A picture is the number of its name in `Texture.pak` (`0x6569C0`, `0x657360`: the name's hashes, whatever its case, `0x6614C0`), 0 for a name the pak does not hold; a step with the number 0 and no ripple lays nothing (`0x639822`).
- **Confirmed** on the installation's files: `PAK/texture.pak` holds `footstepdesert` and `footstepsnow`, 32 × 32 texels, and `footstepwatera_01` to `05` and `footstepwaterb_01` to `05`, 64 × 64; no pak holds a swamp's print, and `Sacred.exe` names the `B` ripples nowhere.
  So both waters take the `A` ripples, the `B` series is never drawn, and a step on a swamp lays nothing.
- The two prints are one shape in two colours (**confirmed** on the converted textures): an upright oval sole of 16 × 27 texels in the texture's middle (the columns 8 to 23, the rows 1 to 27), with neither toes nor a heel, so that it reads alike for either foot and either way round; its alpha is 255 over the sole and falls at its rim alone, so a print covers the ground under it.
  The ripples' frames are faint: their alphas reach 136 to 238 and average 6 to 19 over a frame.
- The bytes `+0xF7` and `+0xF8` of the sector's record choose no picture: the step reads the cell's liquid material only to leave the ripple out on a drained cell (the material 13, docs/re/sound.md, "Footprints"; "The liquid cells" above).
- On a bridge over the water the step still lays the ripple, at the deck's height: the bridge's test changes the nature of the sound alone (docs/re/sound.md, "Footsteps by the ground").
- Where a creature can step on them (**confirmed** on the sector files, the cells whose low nibble is neither 1 nor 2, docs/02 §5.2): 948,560 cells of `DESERT` in 583 sectors, 66,399 of `ARCTIC` in 106, 186,090 of `WATERA` in 866 and 8,891 of `WATERB` in 50, of which 9,812 and 368 pass the bridge's test, and 494 of `SUMPF` in 9.

**The slots.**
The world (`0xAD3560`) holds 64 slots of `0xCC` bytes from its `+0x842C`:

| Offset | Field |
|---|---|
| `+0x00` | the flags: bit 0 in use, bit 4 a ripple; the step writes 1 for a print and `0x11` for a ripple |
| `+0x04` | a print's picture |
| `+0x08` | a ripple's pictures, up to eight, the unused 0 |
| `+0x28` | a ripple's frame, a float from 0 |
| `+0x2C`, `+0x30` | its point on the screen at zoom 1, two integers |
| `+0x34` | the creature's cell: the sector, the global cell's `x` and `y`, the floor |
| `+0x44` | the frames' count at its making (`0xCD7A50`, which the render thread's timer raises by one a frame, `0x641520`) |
| `+0x48` | its four vertices, written at each draw |
| `+0xC8` | the heading, a float in degrees |

- The step takes the first slot not in use, else the slot of the smallest count, the oldest (`0x639917` to `0x63993B`), and fills it (`0x41EA30` a print, `0x41EAF0` a ripple).
- It sets bit `0x10` of the cell's flags (`+0x1E`), which no cell of the files holds (docs/02 §5.2): the cell holds a footprint.
- The point (`0x622620`): `x' = 48·(x − y)/53.66563` and `y' = 24·(x + y)/53.66563 − h`, truncated, `(x, y)` the creature's place and `h` the height it stands at there (`0x636520`, with the floor's `+0x33` times 28 on a building's floor; docs/re/movement.md, "The height"): where the creature's own origin is drawn, on the bed of a water, on the deck of a bridge.
- A save holds the 64 slots (`0x41EF70`, from `0x63B580`), and a load gives each the count of its moment, a full life again.

**The draw.**
- The rows' walk (`0x62AE90`, at `0x62B7FF`) appends each cell it passes whose flags hold bit `0x10` and whose nature is `DESERT`, `ARCTIC`, `WATERA` or `WATERB` to a vector of the view (`+0x86884`, entries of 16 bytes, emptied before the rows, `0x6325A9`).
  `SUMPF` is not among the four: a swamp's print would be kept in a slot and never drawn, were its picture in the pak.
- The lists' walk (`0x62FF60`, at `0x6300DD` to `0x630136`) calls `0x639B50` for each cell of the vector before its first list, after the world's render has turned the depth test on (`0x63289D`).
- `0x639B50` walks the slots from the first and hands the first one whose cell (`x` and `y` alone, `0x41EBF0`) is the cell's to the slot's draw (`0x41EC20`), in use or not, and no other: one slot a cell a frame.
  Where no slot holds the cell it clears the cell's bit `0x10`.
- The slot's draw makes a quad of four vertices already on the screen (`XYZRHW`, the depth 0, a diffuse colour and a texture's corner each; a strip of two triangles):
  - its corners `(p, q)` are `(±17, ±17)`, the texture's `u` 0 at `p` −17 and 1 at 17, its `v` 0 at `q` −17 and 1 at 17;
  - 24 is taken from each `q`, so that the square of 34 lies from 7 to 41 ahead of the point, its middle 24 ahead and the texture's top edge the far one;
  - it is turned by `a`, the heading less 90°, and flattened by half: `X = p·cos a + (q − 24)·sin a` to the right and `Y = ((q − 24)·cos a − p·sin a)/2` down the screen, so a creature heading up the screen has the square above its point, the texture's left on its left, and one heading right has it to the right;
  - set at the slot's point less the camera's (`0xAD7998`, `0xAD799C`: the view's `+0x14` and `+0x18` with the weather's jolt, taken from the screen's middle (512, 384)), and scaled about the screen's middle by the zoom (`+0xB60`).
- So the picture is a square of 34 pixels across the screen and 17 down it at zoom 1 when the creature heads along a screen's axis: a square lying flat on the ground, of the side `34·√(5/8)` = 26.88 world units (half a cell's side and a little), its middle `24·√(5/8)` = 18.97 world units ahead of the creature's place along the square's own axis, at the one height of that place, the ground's slope not followed.
  A print's sole takes 17 by 28.7 of the square's 34 by 34, 13.4 world units across and 22.7 along, the same for every creature, and a ripple is drawn into the same square.
- The turn is the direction's angle on the screen used as an angle on the ground: for a creature facing `(dx, dy)` on the grid the square's axis lies along `(3dx − dy, 3dy − dx)` and its right side along `(dx − 3dy, 3dx − dy)`.
  That is the creature's own direction along the screen's four axes, and between them it leans toward the screen's level, by 18.4° for one that walks along a grid's axis and by 19.5° at the most.
- The bits 2 and 4 of the flags would move the square 6 to the creature's left or right (`0x41EDE6`, `0x41EDF5`), and nothing sets them: both feet's prints lie on the creature's own line.
- The colour of all four corners is the light at the slot's point (`0x41B510`, docs/re/daynight.md, "The light"): white by day, the night's and the dungeons' light else; its alpha is 255 through its life.
- The states (`0x643430`): the blending on (`4`); a print blended by its alpha (`SRCALPHA`, `INVSRCALPHA`); a ripple's alpha added to the frame (`SRCALPHA`, `ONE`, the flag `0x10`); the filter as the liquids' pass left it, linear (`0x800`); the depth test on, which every vertex passes at its depth 0 (**hypothesis**: the depth's write is off, as the frame's start set it, `0x632560`, since a print written at the depth 0 would hide the models drawn after it).
- A ripple draws the picture of its frame's whole part, and after each draw its frame grows by seven times the frame's time (`0x41EF29`: the timer's `+0x0C`, the smoothed real time of a frame): five pictures in 0.71 s, then the slot is free (the frame at 8, or at a picture of 0).

**The life.**
- At a slot's draw, once more than 500 frames have been counted since its making, its bit 0 is cleared (`0x41EC49`), after which it is drawn that one frame more: a print stands 500 frames of the render thread, 8.3 s at 60 frames a second and twice that at 30, with no fade, and is gone at once.
- Nothing else ends a print: one whose cell is off the screen, or behind another slot of its cell, keeps its slot until the step takes it as the oldest.

**One a cell.**
As the draw takes the first slot that names a cell and stops, of two slots that name one cell only the one that comes first among the 64 is drawn, and only it ages; the other waits, whether the first is still in use or not, until the step takes the first for another cell.
- A print that waited is by then mostly past its 500 frames: it shows for a single frame and ends.
- A ripple that waited plays its five frames then, late and behind the walker, as its frame did not move on while it waited: where the first ripple ended and the next step elsewhere took its slot, some 0.7 s after its own step.
- The slots fill from the first, so it is mostly the later step into a cell that waits.

A running Seraphim crosses five and a half cells a second (294 world units, docs/re/movement.md, "The speed"), so most of her steps fall in a cell of their own, and a slow walker's one or two a cell (**estimate**: the clips' lengths between two steps are not measured).

**The swamp.**
A step on `SUMPF` shows nothing, twice over: the pak holds no `FOOTSTEPSWAMP.TGA`, so no slot is taken, and the rows' walk appends no cell of that nature.

**What frames must settle** (the hero walking over sand, snow, a swamp and through the start's pond, #3066):
- the print's size, place and turn as computed: 34 by 17 pixels at zoom 1, ahead of the walker's point, and askew on a walk along a grid's axis;
- its life, 500 frames whatever the frame rate, and its end without a fade;
- one print a cell, and the late ripples behind a walker in the water;
- the ripple over the water's surface and under the walker, brighter for being added, and whether one shows about a bridge's deck, whose sprites the lists draw over it;
- nothing on a swamp, and the same prints behind an NPC or a monster as behind the hero, none behind an animal or a ghost;
- the night's light on a print.

Places for them (**confirmed** on the sector files, every cell one a creature can step on): the start sector 50,39 has 76 such cells of `WATERA`, 17 of them under the bridge's test, as the row of the global cells (3214 to 3227, 2536), and two of `SUMPF`, (3251, 2551) and (3251, 2552); the nearest snow is in sector 49,44, about the cell (3150, 2830), and the nearest sand in sector 50,51, about (3250, 3300).

`opensacred-game` lays them since #3066 (`footprints`, `opensacred_rules::particles::{Print, lays_footprints, footprint_axes}`, `steps`, `sound::HeroStep`): at each step of a creature on the ground whose type lays them, the hero's but in a winged form or of set 23's fires, none for a creature aloft or the Big Demon, a square of 26.9 world units flat at its height, its middle 19 ahead, turned as above, the prints laid over by their alpha for 8.3 s of the game's time and the ripples added, their five frames at 7 a second and gone after the last (**hypothesis**), 64 at most and one to a cell, in list 0 under every sprite and model and tinted by the night (docs/decisions/drawing.md, "Footprints stand 8.3 s of the game's time", "One footprint a cell, none waiting").
**Seen** 2026-11-22 in the game: the hero's trail of prints on the snow of 49,44 and on the sand of 50,51; the original's frames wait for its driver.

## Buildings: masks and layers

A cell whose flags (`+0x1E`) have bit 0 set belongs to a building: for such a cell `0x62AE90` asks `0x6354D0` for the building's object, which follows the cell's two signed offsets at `+0x1C` and `+0x1D` (the "offsets of the parent object" of docs/02 §5.2) to the cell that holds it.
The building's trigger (the object's `+0x27`) selects a 16-byte record of a table of the world (`+0x268` of the object at `+0x04` of the view), whose word at `+0x0A` is a mask of one bit, the building's current state.

## Depth

`0x62FF60` draws a list's sprites piece by piece.
For a sprite whose type (the `items.pak` record's `+0x00` as a u32) has bit `0x200`, it switches two render states of the engine's state table (`0x100` and `0x1`, the depth write and the depth test: `0x643470` sets `D3DRS_ZWRITEENABLE` and `D3DRS_ZENABLE` for them, confirmed 2026-10-18) on and gives each of the quad's four vertices the depth that `0x6288A0` computes from the vertex's screen y and a height: the static's byte at `+0x33` (Static.PAK) times 28.
`0x6288A0` returns the depth of the point at that screen y lying that high above the ground (docs/02 §5.3), so the quad lies in a horizontal plane at that height: what stands below it is hidden, what stands above shows.
For every other sprite the two states are off and the depth is 0: it is drawn over what is there, in the order of its list.

In the game data 47 types carry bit `0x200`, with 220 drawn objects in the whole world; by their editor names they are the floors of towers' and houses' upper storeys.
Their types lack bit `0x4`, so the floors go to list 3, among the sprites by their rows (counted on the sector files 2026-10-19, #143).
Every floor's object belongs to its building's part of its storey ("Buildings: masks and layers"), 203 parts in all, and lies in the first rows of the part: the first floor of a part 2 rows after the part's first (the median; 0 to 51).
So the client draws nearly all of a part's sprites after its floor, 7,664 of the 8,121 sprites and miniatures of those parts, the floors aside, and draws them over the floor whole.
4,205 of them reach further down the screen than half a cell below their cell's point on the floor, which the floor's plane, nearer there than their cells, would hide in a depth test: the four beds by the left wall of the house of sector 52,39 by 24 to 49 pixels, its carpet by 128, the top of its art at its cell's point on the floor.
So do 574 of the 1,356 shadows drawn with them (entries of kind `0x11`, below).

Read again 2026-10-19 (#120): `0x6288A0` returns `(2 · zoom · (200 − y · 400/768) − h / sin(atan ½) + b) / 3100 + 0.002` for the screen's `y` and the height `h`, `b` the renderer's `+0x970AC`, which the world view's constructor (`0x624A00`) sets to the distance from the camera to the point it looks at, `√(1200² + 600²)`, plus 600: the depth of the models' own projection (`ortho(±267, ±200, −600, 2500)` under the view of `0x6284E0`, which looks down at atan(1/2) from 1,200 units back and 600 up), of the point at that height in the models' frame.
So the plane is at `h` units of the models' height: `+0x33` 4, 112 units, lies 192 pixels above the ground at zoom 1 (a unit of height `768/400 · 2/√5` pixels, 1.72).
The 220 floors' `+0x33` are 4 (126 of them), 5 (39), 8 (34), 6, 7, 12, 14, 16 and 21.
A building's floor objects (its static of flag `0x10`'s children, docs/re/movement.md) carry the same byte: 0 for every floor 1, 3 to 10 for floor 2 (mostly 3 and 4), 8 to 28 for floor 3, 21 for the one floor 4 that has it (counted over the 1,245 buildings 2026-10-19).
The grids of a building's floors lie at one place (the two floors of the house of sector 52,39 at (3328, 2519)), so the cells of floor 2 lie over those of floor 1.
A creature on a building's floor stands at its floor object's height, `+0x33 · 28` units, above the second heights of the floor's grid, so the floor's plane does not hide it (**confirmed** by code 2026-10-19, #141, docs/re/movement.md, "The height").

The other states of that table (`0x643470`, the engine's switch of Direct3D 7 states, through `IDirect3DDevice7::SetRenderState` and `SetTextureStageState` at `+0x50` and `+0x94` of the device's table; read again 2026-10-18): `8` and `0x10` make the source and the destination blend factors `ONE` (else `SRCALPHA` and `INVSRCALPHA`), `0x40` the depth function `GREATER` (else `LESSEQUAL`; only `cGranny::renderShadowFake`, `0x4073B0`, sets it, for a second pass of a model's fake shadow), `0x800` the linear filter of both texture stages (else point), `0x2` the culling of counter-clockwise faces (else none), `0x4` the alpha blending, `0x20` the lighting, `0x4000` the alpha test.

## Blending

Read 2026-10-18 (#35).
The device's setup (`0x643190`) puts the alpha test's reference at `0x40` and its function at `GREATER`, turns the alpha blending on and the alpha test off.
Before a sprite's pieces `0x62FF60` turns the lighting and the culling off, the blend factors to `SRCALPHA` and `INVSRCALPHA`, the filter to point and the alpha test on (`0x630367`…`0x6303AA`); after them, the depth states and the alpha test off again (`0x63098F`…`0x6309B0`).
So the original drops a sprite's texels of alpha 64 or less, the atlases' 4-bit alphas 0 to 3 (`MIXnnnn.444`, docs/02 §3.3), and blends the rest by their alpha over what is drawn, in the order of its lists, a texel to a pixel at zoom 1.
A miniature (`0x630F2C`…) is blended by its alpha too, without the test, which the sprite's path leaves off; it is filtered linearly while the zoom is over 1.2, else by point (`0x630F5B`, and `0x62F321` for one of a liquid cell, `0x62E410`).
The zoom is the world view's float at `+0xB60`, which the two setters of the zoom (`0x628130` and `0x628310`) keep within 0.5 to 2 and which scales the projection, 2 the widest view (docs/02 §5.3, docs/re/ai.md); so the miniatures are filtered linearly in the wider views, where a texel of the art covers less than 5/6 of a pixel, and by point at 1.2 and closer (read again 2026-10-20, #343).
The ground takes the same switch: `0x62AE90` sets the state `0x800` by the same test against 1.2 at the head of each row, before the row's quads (`0x62AF32`; docs/02 §5.4).

## The statics' shadows

Read 2026-10-18 and 2026-10-19 (#100).
The shadows are not painted into the sprites: the trees' and the bushes' sprites have no half-transparent texels.
The world view's constructor (`0x624430`) loads `SHADOW_TREE00.TGA` (`texture.pak` record 14942, 512 × 512, black, its alpha up to 170) into `+0x97070` of the view, a grid of 16 × 16 silhouettes of 32 × 32 texels (trees, bushes, posts, fences, rocks, round and oblong blobs), and fills a table at `+0x30` with the texture coordinates of each cell's top-left corner, `(column / 16, row / 16)` for cell `16 · row + column`.

`0x62AE90` appends an entry of kind 8 to list 1 for every static whose type has bit `0x10000` (`items.pak +0x00` as a u32), that lacks flag `0x800`, whose mask is 1, while `DETAILLEVEL` is 1 or more (`0x62BA03`).
It does so before it tests the building's state or looks for the static's sprite, so the static need not be drawn, and a static of a liquid cell sends its shadow to list 1 too.
A sprite of such a type with a mask of 2 or more, the buildings' furniture, goes to its list as an entry of kind `0x11` instead of 1 (docs/02 §6.1): drawn with its building's part.

`0x62FF60` draws an entry of kind 8 with `0x631EB0` at the entry's screen position, the static's position, the sprite's top-left corner (`0x6301FF`); for an entry of kind `0x11` it draws the shadow the same way right before the sprite (`0x63025B`); `0x62E410` does the same for the entries of the liquid cells.
`0x631EB0` draws one strip of four vertices (FVF `0x1C4`: a transformed position, a diffuse and a specular colour, one pair of texture coordinates) from the static's type's fields (docs/02 §6.1): the cell `c` (`+0x5B`), the foot `(2·dx, 2·dy)` from the halves at `+0x5D` and `+0x5F`, the shape (`+0x63`) and the size `s` (`+0x64`), all in pixels at zoom 1 from the static's position, scaled by the zoom about the screen's centre.
Its top-left and top-right corners lie at `2dy − s`, from `2dx − s` to `2dx + s` for the shape 0 and from `2dx` to `2dx + 2s` for any other, the shadow leaning up and to the right; its bottom-left and bottom-right corners at `2dy`, from `2dx − s` to `2dx + s`.
The four corners take the cell's corners of the atlas in the same order, so the whole cell stretches over the quad.
The depth test and the depth write are off, the blending `SRCALPHA` and `INVSRCALPHA`, the filter linear (`0x643430` with `0x100`, `1`, `8`, `0x10` off and `0x800` on), and the alpha test as the list pass leaves it between two sprites, off.
Every vertex is white with an alpha of `255 − N` (`0x41B9E0`), `N` the night's level (docs/re/daynight.md): the shadows fade out over the dusk, are gone by night and come back over the dawn, in a dungeon too, where `N` follows the clock as well.

On the data: 2,043 types have the bit, 1,635 of them leaning and 408 rectangles (docs/02 §6.1); 356,356 statics are of those types, 347,123 of them of mask 1 and 9,232 of a mask of 2 or more; 6,195 have flag `0x800`.
In the starting sector 50,39, 267 statics carry a shadow: bushes, fences, benches, beds, posts, braziers, trees, rocks, chairs, chandeliers.

## The trees about the heroes

Read 2026-10-18.
As `0x62FF60` draws a sprite whose type has bit `0x10` (4,544 types: the 4,533 of type 25, the trees, and 11 floors of type 29), it fades the vertices of each piece that lie near the points of a list at `+0x96B3C` of the renderer (`+0x96FEC` of them, records of 12 bytes: the point's x and y on the screen as two i16, a counter that each faded vertex raises, and a float `k`).
For a vertex within `R / zoom` of a point across and down, `d` is its distance to the point (an integer square root of `dx² + dy²`), `t = min(d · zoom / R, 1)`, and the vertex's alpha becomes `G · (1 − k) + (255 − G) · t`, truncated (`0x630761` to `0x6307B4`): `G · (1 − k)` at the point and `255 − k · G` from `R / zoom` on to the edge of that box, whose corners lie `√2 · R / zoom` from the point, while a vertex outside the box keeps the alpha 255 its colour was given (`0x630576`, `0x630602`).
So while `k` is 0 a tree goes transparent about the point and stays whole away from it; once `k` has risen (below) it stays `k · G` short of whole up to the box's edge, and steps to whole there (read again 2026-11-24 for #3292).
`R` and `G` are the floats at `0xAD4DF0` and `0xAD4DF4`, the balance keys `TransSize` and `TransValu`: `Balance.bin` holds them, 190.0 and 50.0 (docs/02 §6.3), so a tree is at an alpha of 50 out of 255 at the hero and whole 190 pixels from it at zoom 1.
The list is emptied before the rows (`0x6325C8`) and filled as `0x62E410` and `0x62FF60` pass the creatures on the screen, each at the middle of its rectangle there (`0x44CF20`, the chooser's, docs/re/cursor.md), with `k` 0 and its counter 0 (`0x631644`, `0x631654`).
It holds 99 points, not 100 (read 2026-11-24 for #3292 in the code, **hypothesis** until seen): the count stops at 99 (`0x631665`, `0x62FA08`), so each creature past the 99th is written into the hundredth record, which no loop reads.
Read again 2026-11-21 for #2942 (`0x631485` to `0x6315EA`, **confirmed** by code), a creature adds a point where it is:

- the local player's hero (an object of id up to 16 that is the local player's, `0x7D84A0`);
- a horse (`0x4266F0`: its creature's record's class 4) whose `+0x1EC`, its rider, is the local player;
- a creature whose master (`+0x251`) is the local player, of the flags `0x104` of `+0x1F4`, found in the list of 9-byte records at `+0x39C` of `0x603E30`'s object (**hypothesis**: the hero's followers, its portraits' column);
- a creature hostile to the local player (`0x549000`: the player's AI state not 9 and its action not 6, its `+0x4D8` not 0, and the sides' hostility) that holds a ranged weapon in a hand (`0x54A2A0`);
- or a creature that fights: the AI's state 4 or 8 with a target at `+0x104`, or the action 3 or 5 or the AI's state 11 or 6 with one at `+0x158`, the test of a fight that the followers' dismissal makes (docs/re/ai.md).

So a tree fades about the creatures drawn before it in the frame, not about one drawn after it, and a vertex within the boxes of two points takes the alpha of the later one in the list, the points' order of drawing, and raises the counters of both.

`k` is not 0 for long (read 2026-11-21 for #2942 and again 2026-11-24 for #3292, in `0x62FF60`'s path of a sprite from `0x630400` to `0x630A2A`; **hypothesis** until the running original shows `k`, which the probes below never read).
The sprite's place and its pieces' corners are scaled by the zoom about the screen's middle, 512 and 384 (the floats at `0x8906FC` and `0x8909F0`), and each piece is tested against the screen, 1,024 × 768 (`0x8906F4`, `0x892040`), before anything else: a piece wholly off it is neither drawn nor faded nor counted (`0x6304E6` to `0x63053C`, which skip to the loop's end at `0x63095E`).
Of a sprite of a type of bit `0x10`, each corner of a piece on the screen that lies in a point's box takes its alpha and raises that point's counter (`0x6307BB`); the type is tested inside the pieces' loop (`0x63061D`) and decides only whether a piece's corners fade.
After the last piece the loop (`0x630976`) runs on into the batch's flush and the states' reset (`0x63097C` to `0x6309B0`) and from there, with no test of the type, into the update (`0x6309B5` to `0x630A2A`): every point whose counter is not 0 takes `k += counter / (4 × the sprite's pieces)`, the pieces counted whole, on the screen or not, and `k` over 0.8 is set to 0.8, the float at `0x890820` (**confirmed** on its bytes, `0x3F4CCCCD`).
So `k` rises after every sprite of the lists that `0x62FF60` draws, an entry of kind 1 or `0x11` whatever its type, a bush, a wall or a rock as well as a tree, not after the fading ones alone; the miniatures, the shadows of kind 8 and the objects take other paths, which leave it alone: `k` (`+0x96B44` of the renderer) is written only where a point is added and in this update, and in `0x62E410`'s copies of both (`0x62F9F7`, `0x62ED85`), whose update follows each sprite that function draws in the same way.
A sprite of no pieces would divide by 0 there, as `0x630468` sends it to the flush at once, but none gets so far: a sprite of `mixed.pak` without pieces has no array of them either (`0x41FA30` resizes its empty vector to 0, which allocates nothing, `0x420680`, `0x420E90`), so `0x41FFB0` answers 0 and `0x630361` leaves the sprite before its states.
The counter is set to 0 with `k` as its point is added and only raised after, never cleared in the frame; it counts only corners on the screen while the divisor counts every piece, so `k` depends on what the original's 1,024 × 768 screen holds.
The sprite that first fades about a point fades with `k` 0, and `k` takes at once the share of all its corners, on the screen or not, that lay in the box; each later sprite of `n` pieces adds the corners faded about the point so far over `4n`, so that four corners in the box bring `k` to 0.8 by the first later sprite of one piece, or by at most eight later sprites of ten pieces.
So wherever other sprites lie between the first tree to fade about a point and the next, `k` reaches 0.8 within a few of them, and the next tree fades with a `k` short of 0.8 only where the first barely grazes the box or the next follows it straight in the order of drawing (**hypothesis**, a consequence of the reading).
At 0.8 a tree fades to `(1 − k) · G` at the point, 9 out of 255 (9.9999994 with the float 0.8, which `_ftol`, `0x84A1F4`, truncates), and to `255 − k · G` from `R / zoom` on to the box's edge, 214 at the 53 bits that docs/01 §7 gives the render thread and 215 in single precision, against 255 just outside the box.

## The models

The world's dynamic objects (the creatures, the start script's objects) are entries of the same draw lists.
After a cell's chain of statics, the "wobj" walk of `0x62AE90` takes the objects of its building's floor that the state shows (of every floor for an open building, flag `0x400`), if any, then those of the cell's own chain, layer 0 (above), and appends each as an entry of kind 0 with the object's index.
It walks each chain twice (read 2026-11-21 for #2941, **confirmed** by code: `0x62C337` … `0x62C633` every floor of an open building, `0x62C74E` … `0x62CA88` the floor the state shows, `0x62CAEB` … `0x62CF0E` the cell's own chain):

- The first pass takes the objects that are no FX objects (`0x4280B0`: the type's category, `items.pak +0x2E`, is not 12), each to list 3 (the vector at `+0x96AF4` of the view), or in a liquid cell to the liquid cells' vector (above).
- The second takes the FX objects alone: one of a type of the flag `0x800000` (`0x4280F0`, bit 23 of `items.pak +0x00`) to list 4; one of the flag `0x4` (`0x4280D0`) to list 0, on a building's floor only while the object's floor (its position's byte `+0x24`, docs/re/movement.md) is 0, else to list 3; any other to list 3.

So in a cell every FX object of list 3 is drawn after the cell's other objects, whatever the chain's order.
Of the 210 FX types (docs/re/effects.md, "The FX objects"), three have the flag `0x800000`, the smoke class's variants 1 to 3 over chimneys and braziers (771, 810, 811, flags `0x801002`), and five the flag `0x4`, the five of kind 6 on the ground (779, the marks; 795; 903, a blow's splashes; 907, a body's puddle; 918, a summoning's pentagram); none has both, and of the other types only statics' of the category 1 have either (of the kinds 9, 12, 13, 25 and 29; counted on `tables/items.ron` 2026-11-21).
So the chimneys' smokes lie over every sprite and model of the other lists, and the ground's five under the statics' shadows, the underlays of list 2 and every sprite and model of lists 3 and 4, among the underlays of list 0 by their rows.
`0x62FF60` walks the five lists in order (the vectors from `+0x96AD0`, 12 bytes apart) and draws an entry of kind 0 by calling the object's own draw (its virtual function at `+0x14`), in its place among the list's sprites; an entry of the kind `0x20`, a hidden stash's, is its blink (docs/re/containers.md, "The hidden stashes").
So a model is drawn after every sprite of an earlier row and of an earlier cell of its row, and after its cell's statics; the sprites, without the depth test, cover a model drawn before them whole and lie whole under one drawn after them.

The states of a model's own draw (read 2026-10-22, #516, **confirmed** by code and in the running original): `cGranny::render` (`0x405C90`) turns on, through the engine's switch (`0x643430`, the states above), the depth test (`1`, `ZENABLE`), the lighting (`0x20`), the culling of counter-clockwise faces (`2`), the alpha blending (`4`) with the factors `SRCALPHA` and `INVSRCALPHA` (`8` and `0x10` off), the depth function `LESSEQUAL` (`0x40` off), the linear filter (`0x800`) and the alpha test (`0x4000`), which it turns off again after the model.
The depth write (`0x100`, `ZWRITEENABLE`) is on, unless the top bit of its last argument asks for it off; the rest of that argument, when not 0, names a second texture, which the second stage blends over the first by its own alpha (`0x20000`).
The first texture stage modulates the texture's alpha by the diffuse's (`0x2000`, which the device's setup turns on, `0x643190`): a hook on `0x405C90` in the running original found the state on at all 1,088 models drawn in 3 s at the Seraphim's start.
At 300 of the models' draw calls (`DrawIndexedPrimitiveStrided` at `0x4061BC`) the device itself answered so: the alpha test on, its reference 64 and its function `GREATER`; the blending on, `SRCALPHA` and `INVSRCALPHA`; the depth test and write on, `LESSEQUAL`; the culling counter-clockwise; the first stage's colour and alpha the texture's times the diffuse's, filtered linearly without mipmaps; the second stage off; and the material's diffuse alpha 1, which the lighting gives the vertices, whose format (`0x112`) has no colour of its own.
So the original drops a model's texels of alpha 64 or less, filtered linearly, and blends the rest by their alpha over what is drawn, while writing their depth.
In the running original the blacksmith of (3253, 2867) shows the front flap of his apron cut to a strip under the belt, where his texture's two strokes of alpha 0 lie (docs/02 §4.6).
`opensacred-game` tests a model's texels so (`models::CUTOFF`, #516), and blends the translucent ones that the test keeps in a second pass (#519, "What the game does with it" below).

A script's object is in the list of its own cell, wherever its model is drawn (**confirmed** by code and in the running original 2026-10-22, #459):

- `CreateObj`'s handler (`0x485C10`) puts the object at its `0x04` cell's centre, and with a floor there `sdk_CREATE` (`0x5FBA40`) sets its layer (`+0x2C`) to that floor's record (`0x6354D0`, `0x4498F0`); `0x5FCCB0` then chains it into the list of the cell at its position on its layer (the cell's `+0x08`, the next object at the object's `+0x30`), which the walk above takes.
- The same handler, for an object with a model and a world position `0x20`, takes the object's height `h` at its cell's centre on its layer in units of the models' height (`0x449950`: the layer's cell's second heights times 2.5, plus the floor's `+0x33` times 28), turns the models' vector `(0, 2h, 0)` into the grid's plane (`0x623AD0`), `1.92 · h` world units along x and along y, and sets the floats `+0xCC`, `+0xD0` to `0x20` plus those and `+0xD4` to `h`, with the object's flag `0x800000`.

## The models' turn

Read 2026-10-19 (#159).
An object keeps its direction at `+0x70`, a vector on the grid's plane, and `0x44DBF0` turns its model by it: it takes the object's position and the position moved by the direction to the screen (`0x6239C0`, the cells' projection `48 (x − y)`, `24 (x + y)` pixels a cell, then back through the models' view), normalises their difference to `(a, b)` in the models' frame (`x` right on the screen, `y` toward the viewer) and sets the model's orientation (`GrannySetSequenceOrientationM`) to the rows `(a, b, 0)`, `(−b, a, 0)`, `(0, 0, 1)`: the model's `x` points along the direction.
It does so at once when the object has its model (`+0x14` bit 26), and the model's load (`0x44C330`, the object's virtual function at `+0x28`, which its first draw calls) does it again from `+0x70`.
The mirror of the models' middle axis as the client loads them (docs/02 §4.1) does not show on the screen: a guard facing the viewer holds his sword in his right hand, on the screen's left, in the original as in `opensacred-game`, whose models are not mirrored (**confirmed** 2026-10-22, #445; which of the view and the turn undoes it is not read).

- A creature on the move turns by the vector of its step on the grid (`0x5941A0` through `0x44DD90`, at `0x5992A6`), so it faces where it goes.
- A heading in degrees (`0x44DDC0`, which `CreateNPC` and `CreateObj` call) is an angle on the screen: `(cos, sin)` of it, 0° to the right and 90° down, goes back through the inverse of the cells' projection (`0x622660`, the 2 × 2 matrix of `0x6239C0` inverted) into the grid's plane, so the model shows at that angle on the screen.
  Read 2026-10-19 (#161): static initialisers set the matrix from the constants at `0x892048`, 53.66563 (a cell's side in world units), 48.0 and 24.0: `0x622580` the grid's `x` to `(48, 24) / 53.66563` on the screen, `0x6225B0` its `y` to `(−48, 24) / 53.66563`, and `0x6225F0` the inverse of the determinant, `1 / 0.8 = 1.25` (`0xAD7788`).
  So `0x622660` takes the screen's `(cos, sin)` to `1.25 · (0.447 cos + 0.894 sin, 0.894 sin − 0.447 cos)` on the grid, the direction `(cos + 2 sin, 2 sin − cos)`; the degrees become radians by the constant at `0x8909F8`, π/180.
- As the object manager makes an object (`0x5FB530`), it sends it the event `0x40` with the float at `+0x57` of its `items.pak` record (docs/02 §6.1) at the event's `+0x10` (`0x5FB726`), which `cObject3D`'s receive (`0x44BCD0`) loads as a float and turns into its direction the same way, through `0x622660`: the angle on the screen an object faces until something turns it, −30° for the chests, barrels and book stands of the start scripts.
- `CreateObj`'s handler (`0x485C10`) turns the object by its heading only when it is above 0, so a script's object without one keeps its item's angle; `CreateNPC`'s takes a random heading without one (docs/02 §7.3).

The maintainer's screenshots of the original agree (2026-10-19): the black chest by the chapel's pews (no heading, `chest.grn`'s −30°) and the wooden chest of a house of sector 50,40 (heading 330) stand alike, their long sides up and to the right, and the chest by the chapel's outer corner (heading 20) almost level; `opensacred-game` drew them turned along the grid's axes before, a quarter turn off for the first two.

## The view's point

Read 2026-11-25 for #3730 (**confirmed** by code unless marked otherwise) and **seen** in the running original the same day (the driver of #448, a Seraphim's new game in god mode, probes of `0x610449`, `0x624FC0` and `0x62A5C0`; the frames kept in the scratchpad).

The world view (`cWorldView`, its draw's class `cWorldView0` of the vtable `0x8920F8`; the engine's `+0x08` table at its `+0x48`) keeps a point of the iso plane, the screen's plane at zoom 1, in which the cell (x, y) has its middle at (48 (x − y), 24 (x + y)) (docs/02 §5.3).
Its fields: the current point at `+0x14` and `+0x18`, the point asked at `+0x1C` and `+0x20`, an offset at `+0x24` and `+0x28`, and the size of the plane it shows at `+0x96AB0` and `+0x96AB4`, 1,024 and 768 times the zoom (`+0xB60`), truncated (`cWorldView_updateViewMetrics`, `0x624A00`).
The world's draw centres the screen on the current point, the weather's jolt added (`0xAD7998`, "The footprints" above; docs/re/weather.md), and the overview's lens takes the current point without the jolt (`0x6113C0`, `0x6113D0`; docs/re/maps.md, "The overview map"): it is the camera of the world and of the overview alike.

- **The follow** (`0x610380`, at each frame of the render thread, from `0x60E765`): while the engine's `+0x54` has the bit 4 and not `0x800`, it takes the local hero's point (`0x44D820`), its place carried to the iso plane and raised by its height through the models' projection, about 1.717 pixels a unit of the models' height, the height being the ground's or the floor's under it (`0x449950`; docs/re/movement.md, "The height").
  It asks the view (through `cEngine_scrollViewAndWarpCursor`, `0x6105B0`) for the point halfway from the current point to the hero's, each axis's half truncated toward 0; where a half passes 256 on either axis, or the engine's `+0x54` has the bit 2 (which it then clears), it asks for the hero's point itself.
  A creature of the `+0x14` bit `0x4000000` in the AI's state 1 with the sub-state 4 or 5 and the action 0 (docs/re/ai.md) gives its bone `Bip01`'s place instead (when that holds is not read).
  Without the bit 4 the mouse at the screen's edges scrolls the view instead (`0x625180`), a free view that a game does not use (**hypothesis**).
- **Seen**: the view closes half the distance at each drawn frame and stands within a pixel of its goal once the hero stands (its x 34,811, 34,815, 34,820, 34,824 behind a hero walking about 5 pixels a frame); on a cell where the hero stood at the height 10.5, its point lay 18.6 pixels above its flat place.
- **The offset** (`0x6250B0`, the engine's event `0xD`, which the equipment window sends, docs/re/inventory.md): the event's two numbers replace the offset and move the point asked by them at once.
  Every point asked then (`cWorldView::setViewPosition`, `0x624FC0`) is the point given plus the offset times the zoom, so that the halving settles on the hero's point plus twice the offset times the zoom, and a jump on the hero's point plus the offset times the zoom: in pixels of the screen, twice the offset at any zoom.
  The equipment window's show gives (100, 40) and its hide (0, −30), and a change of the engine's view (`0x610210`) carries the offset over to the new one.
  **Seen**: the offset (0, 0) in a new game, the view on the hero; with the inventory shown, the view 200 right of the hero and 80 below it, the hero drawn left of and above the screen's middle, clear of the windows; after the hide, the view 60 above the hero until the game was loaded, the hero's feet 60 pixels below the screen's middle (at zoom 2, 118 of the plane's pixels); after a load of a save, the offset (0, 0) again.
  The load's change of the current view carries over no offset, whatever the save held, while the view's points and zoom come back from the save (docs/re/saves.md, "What a load puts back"; read 2026-11-25 for #3739, **confirmed** by code).
- `setViewPosition` leaves the point asked alone where the point given is the current one or the view's `+0x10` has the bit `0x2000`, and where the point given lies in no sector of the world it logs it and leaves it; otherwise it sets the point asked and clears `+0x10`'s bit `0x800`.
- **The update** (`0x62A5C0`, a method of `cWorldView0`, run while `+0x10` has not the bit `0x800`): the view's first placement, before it has a sector (`+0x08`), takes the point asked as it is.
  Later a point asked in no sector is put back to the current one; else the rim's stop below adjusts it, and the view takes it as its current point, each coordinate raised to the next even number where the zoom is above 1, and finds its sector again (docs/02 §5.1).
  Where neither axis may move, it sets `+0x10`'s bit `0x800`, and the view stands until another point is asked.

**The rim's stop.**
A sector's `+0x2E` (`sectors.keyx`'s `+0x1CC`, docs/02 §5.1) has the bit `0x10` where the grid has no sector at (x, y − 1), beyond its upper right side on the screen, `0x20` none at (x + 1, y), its lower right, `0x40` none at (x, y + 1), its lower left, and `0x80` none at (x − 1, y), its upper left (**confirmed** on all 6,050 records).
As the view moves, the update takes the sector of the point asked and those of its neighbours (`+0x04` to `+0x20`) into which a rectangle of the view's size about that point reaches, at most three (`0x629FD0`: the rectangle's corners against the sector's sides; **hypothesis**: every neighbour the rectangle overlaps, the choice at the corners not read to the end).
For each of them, (sx, sy) its middle on the plane and w and h the view's half width and half height, the point asked's axis that moves toward one of the sector's lines is held so:
- moving right, where the view's right edge would pass from left of x = sx − 48 to that line or beyond it, and the sector has `0x10` and `0x20`, or `0x10` alone and the view's top lies above sy, or `0x20` alone and its bottom below sy, the point asked's x becomes sx − 49 − w;
- moving left, the same with the left edge, the line x = sx + 48, the bits `0x80` and `0x40`, and sx + 49 + w;
- moving up, with the top edge and the line y = sy + 24, the bits `0x80` and `0x10`, one of them alone where the view's left edge lies left of sx (`0x80`) or its right edge right of sx (`0x10`), and sy + 25 + w;
- moving down, with the bottom edge and the line y = sy − 24, the bits `0x40` and `0x20` (the left edge left of sx for `0x40`, the right edge right of sx for `0x20`), and sy − 25 − w;
- where that new value is no nearer the line than the current point, the axis stays at the current point and stands.

The up and down stops take the half width where the half height would set the edge on the line, a defect (**confirmed** by code and **seen**): a view that jumps across such a line stands 128 times the zoom farther back than its edge needs, while one that follows a walking hero halts where its edge would first cross, about 24 + h from the sector's middle.
So a view that has come up to a rim sector's middle across one of these lines shows nothing of the sector's outer half, and a hero in that half stands off the screen's middle, off the screen once it is more than 512 times the zoom aside or 384 times it above or below.
It is a stop, not a clamp: a view already beyond a line (placed there at the start, come from the far side, or widened by the zoom) is not brought back, and a view moving away from a line is free.

**Seen** at zoom 1 on the island of the sectors 20 to 22, 94 to 96, whose outer sides all bear the bits:

Its sprite shader brings each one forward by a little in the order above: by its cell's distance along the screen's right, which grows with `cx` along a diagonal, and by its place in the cell's chain (`statics::SpriteMaterial`).
Before, the centre lay at the cell's height, 0.46 units forward per pixel against 0.15 from a cell to the next of its diagonal: a sprite on a cell a third of a pixel higher than the next came over that cell's sprites, as the chapel's back right wall (5 pixels high) over the cabinet against it at the start (#112).
