# The textures' encoding and mipmaps

Rendering and the converter's `build` (`opensacred-convert`, `opensacred-game`), 2026-10-23 (#700).

## The original

Its textures are 16-bit ARGB4444 or 32-bit A8R8G8B8 without mip levels, and the engine makes none: it samples the full texture at any distance (docs/02 §3.1).

## Ours

`build` encodes each texture's PNG master into a KTX2 file (docs/02 §12.2):

* The opaque textures that the GPU samples, the models', the items', the effects' and the loading screens', in BC7 with a full mip chain: BC7 is lossy, within a few steps of 255 a channel, and the levels filter the art where the original would sample it whole.
  These are 85 % of the texels of the textures outside the world's.
* The textures with a texel of alpha under 255 and those whose sides are not multiples of 4 (two), in 8-bit RGBA with a full mip chain, the alpha of each level raised where the share of texels that the models' alpha test keeps would fall short of the largest level's, and never lowered (#816).
* The tile sheets and atlases that the world names and the cursor, which the game composes or reads on the CPU, in 8-bit RGBA of one level, their texels as the masters'.

The ground and the sprites stay as docs/decisions/2026-10-19-linear-filter.md has them: composed per sector on the CPU and filtered linearly without mipmaps.
The models, the items and the effects sample their mip chains trilinearly, Bevy's default sampler.

## Why

BC7 holds a texel in a byte against 8-bit RGBA's 4, in video memory and in the upload, and loads without decoding a PNG; the mipmaps keep the models' textures from shimmering where the camera shrinks them (docs/05 §2), and the maintainer chose BC7 with mipmaps for the game's textures (#694).
The game reads the alpha of a model's texture to decide whether its meshes are drawn in two passes (docs/decisions/2026-10-22-model-alpha.md), which BC7's blocks would hide, so a texture with any alpha below 255 keeps its texels; that also keeps BC7's errors away from the alpha test's edges.
Averaged, the alpha of thin parts, hair, fringes and foliage, falls under the alpha test level by level and they thin out, which raising it keeps away.
Lowering it, where a level's share grows over its edges, took every opaque texel of the level under 255 too, and the game, which blends a model's texels under 255, drew the whole model see-through from afar and faintly so up close (#816); the edge that it would trim is half-transparent and blended already.
A texture that the game composes on the CPU would be decoded from BC7 only to be composed into 8-bit RGBA again, its loss kept and nothing saved; stored as 8-bit RGBA under zstd it is a third smaller than its PNG and decodes four times faster.
BC7 needs whole blocks of 4 × 4 at the largest level, which wgpu requires of a compressed texture.
