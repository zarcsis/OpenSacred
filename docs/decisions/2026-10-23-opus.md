# The sound in Opus

Sound (`opensacred-convert`'s `build`, `opensacred-game`), 2026-10-23 (#701).

## The original

It plays its WAV samples (PCM and IMA ADPCM, mostly 44.1 kHz) as they are, and its MP3 samples (mono, 48 to 128 kbit/s) and music (stereo, 256 kbit/s; the ambient loops 128) through Miles' MP3 decoder (docs/02 §10.2, §10.3).

## Ours

`build` encodes every FLAC master of the sound into Ogg Opus at 48 kHz (docs/02 §12.2):

* a sample at 96 kbit/s mono, 128 stereo;
* a music file at 256 kbit/s stereo, 128 mono.

The masters keep what the original's decoders give (#697), so a sample or a music file that the original stores as MP3 is encoded a second time, its MP3's loss and Opus's together; 72 mono samples of the MP3s' 128 kbit/s get Opus's 96.
The game decodes each file whole as it loads it, as it decoded the FLAC.

## Why

The maintainer chose Opus for the game's sound, over Ogg Vorbis, for its quality at the voices' low bitrates (#694, #701).
Opus at these bitrates is held to be transparent in listening tests, and its 96 kbit/s mono to be above an MP3's 128, so that the second encoding hardly changes what is heard (**estimate**: not checked by listening).
The masters tell no sample's source, so one bitrate a channel count serves every sample, the WAVs' and the MP3s'; the music keeps its MP3s' bitrate.
Opus runs at 48 kHz, and the 44.1 kHz sources are resampled as they are encoded.
