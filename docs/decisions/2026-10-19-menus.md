# The menus

Interface (`opensacred-game`), 2026-10-19 (#172, #178); changed 2026-10-26 (#1118, #1119, #1129, #1130, #1127, #1128).

## The original

The original's main menu and its creation of a hero are screens of its own interface, laid out in code from its textures and atlases (docs/re/menus.md, docs/02 §11), and hold more than a new game.
The main menu is a picture of 12 tiles over moving water, its items in Carolingia: the two campaigns, Multiplayer, Options, Load Game, Extras and Quit.
Its choice of the hero stands the eight classes as idling 3D figures on the pedestals of another such picture, dark until the cursor is on one or it is chosen, over a panel of the hero's name, the difficulty, Import, Back and Start Game, which shows a class's description while the cursor is on its figure.
While a game loads, it shows a loading screen of `PAK/*.bmp` (`ui/screens/loading0.png`, `loading1.png`, docs/02 §11).

## Ours

The main menu is the original's since #1118 (docs/re/menus.md): its picture, its items in their rows, font and gold, the item under the cursor lit with a glow, the items fading in as a menu replaces another, and the Multiplayer and Extras submenus.
Its picture keeps the original's 1024 × 768 scaled to the window's height, black at its sides in a wider window.
Its water moves as the original's since #1127 (docs/re/menus.md, "The water"): the drops fall by its timer of real time, their rings spread a step at its 30 frames a second, and the bed, the reflection and the highlights are drawn by its camera and its formulas (`ripples`); the main menu's first showing reflects the first game's portal, as the original's does.
The menus sound as the original's since #1128 (docs/re/menus.md, "The sound"; `menu_sound`): their music loops from their start through their screens, unless the music is off (`--no-music`), at the volume of the game's music; each drop sounds as it falls; the idle sounds roll every half a second; and a figure that the cursor rests on half a second speaks its class's introduction.
The items whose screens the game does not have yet stand greyed, do nothing and are passed over by the arrows: Options (#1096), Load Game (#1095), Multiplayer's three (M9), and Extras' Credits and movies (#1094).
The game's version stands in the picture's corner where the original's stands.
The Underworld campaign shows its choice of the hero since #1129, every pedestal labelled free and Start Game greyed: its heroes are the saved ones, which the game cannot save, export or import yet (#1095, #1117), so none stands there, not even the two that the installation ships.
Ancaria Campaign shows the original's choice of the hero since #1119: its picture and its water, the figures, and the panel of the original's pieces (`gui_main_12`) and words.
The figures stand, turn, wear and move as the original's since #1130: at the places of the client's table under its camera, in its showcase's items, each playing the original's motion (docs/re/equipment.md, "The choice of the hero's figures").
They stand in a scene of their own, lit from the top left and drawn by their own camera into a texture that the interface lays between the picture and the panel; a figure not lit takes a dark colour and reflects nothing, rather than the original's light, which is not read.
The name starts as the class's own and is carried with the game (`--name`), which shows it nowhere yet; the difficulty is bronze, as the original gives a new hero (docs/decisions/2026-10-26-difficulty.md), and Import stands greyed until the saves (#1095).
The mouse chooses by pointing and clicking, the keyboard with the arrows, Enter and Esc, which goes back a screen; on the choice of the hero the arrows choose a figure, the keys type its name, and Enter starts the game.
While a game loads, the original's first loading screen shows, as high as the window.
In the game, Esc pauses it under a menu of Continue, Main menu and Quit over the frame dimmed, with no saving and no options; the time and the sound stop until it goes on.

## Why

The items that do nothing yet show where the original's stand, so that the menu looks as it will once their screens come.
The water's detail of the settings is not followed: the water is drawn at the installation's detail, every pass, as the game has no such setting yet.
The keyboard's choice is the game's own, for play without a mouse and for the scripts; the play itself does not change.
The figures' light waits for the original's light of the screen; their look does not change what a hero starts with.
