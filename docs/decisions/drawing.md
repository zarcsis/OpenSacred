# Drawing

How the game draws the world: the ground, the sprites, the models and their shadows, the liquids, the night, the effects and the textures (docs/re/drawing.md).

| Decision | Ours, against the original | Why | Kind | Issues |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No puddle on a building's floor | A body that lies on a building's floor lays no blood puddle (907), where the original lays one on the ground under the floor (`0x636520(cell, 0)`). | The original draws it in the list 0, under the building's floor sprites, so that it never shows there (**seen** in the chapel of 50,39); the game's order of the marks would draw it over the floor. | limitation | #2516 |
| Small fire's burnt ground lifted | The burnt ground of the steps' small fire (878) lies half a world unit above the ground, where the original draws it at the ground's height with its depth test (`0x79F7D0`). | The game's ground mesh, at the same height, would hide the quad in part; half a unit shows nowhere in the iso view. | deviation | #1808 |
| Clear waters' second layer per cell | The clear waters' second layer takes each cell's own material's current frame, its quads' corners on their beds' corners, where the original takes the frame that its batcher kept from the frame before and moves the corners 0.2 pixels inward. By night the frame's pass darkens the surfaces with the rest of the world, where the original lights each corner. | The original's frame shows only where two materials meet, a frame behind, and the game's quads share their edges, so the inward fifth of a pixel is not needed. | deviation | #103, #118 |
| Lavas' streaks made opaque | `opensacred-convert textures` gives the non-opaque texels of `A_LAVA`, `D_LAVA` and `E_LAVA` the colour of their column's nearest opaque texel, where the client's blending would show them as grey and purple streaks (**hypothesis**). | The texels are junk of the tool that made them, not a look of the lava, and the play does not change. | repair | #347 |
| Linear filter at every zoom | The ground and every sprite are filtered linearly at any zoom and resolution, each colour weighted by its alpha, where the original point-filters the sprites always and the ground and the miniatures at zoom 1.2 and closer. Where the screen shrinks the sprites, a pixel spanning more than a texel (below 1,440 pixels of height at the widest zooms), their images' levels stand in, the two nearest the texels a pixel spans blended (#839). | Magnified, the point filter's square texels are blocks that shimmer as the camera moves; the maintainer's choice (#42), made in the game against a point and a sharp bilinear filter (#124). Shrunk, one level's filter skips texels, which shimmer as the camera moves in turn. | deviation | #42, #124, #839 |
| Models among the sprites by cell depth | A model's vertices take the depth of the centre of its root's cell plus a hundredth of their own depth from it, in list 3, and a depth prepass keeps only the nearest model's samples, where the original draws a model at its cell's place in the draw lists. | At their true depth the models were cut by every sprite whose plane crossed them (#48), and the prepass orders the models among themselves by their true depths without a second camera (#998). | limitation | #48, #998 |
| Effects at their bearers' places | A system that follows a creature lays its quads no nearer than the depth of the creature's place in the order, 20 world units of its own depth behind the creature's root (**estimate**), its parts further back at their own depths, and the purgatory at its object's, and the Dark Elf's wards' models at one depth behind every texel of a creature standing in their cell (`models::Pass::Under`, #1092), a fire of the world on the ground at one depth after its cell's statics (#2188), and a hidden stash's blink and an event's sparks at one depth after their cell's statics and objects, brought 49 world units toward the camera from their cell's centre on its ground and laid back there (#1519, #2220), and the stargates' whirls and the summonings' triangles and bolts, which the original draws with their depth not tested, at one depth of their place, depth tested (#2220), where the original draws the whole system at its FX object's place in the draw list, before the creature, its billboards writing no depth; a creature on an upper storey's floor keeps its systems no further back than the floor's plane where they show, as its model, and so do the world's fires and the purgatories on such a floor (#2220). | The models lie at their cells' places with a hundredth of their own depths ("Models among the sprites by cell depth"), so a quad behind the back of the creature's body is covered by every texel of its model, and the quads are moved along the line of sight, which the orthographic view leaves where they show. A model on a floor lies no further back than the floor's plane, which a place on the ground lies behind. | limitation | #1519, #2047, #2178, #2188, #2220 |
| Models' shadows by depth, not stencil | The models' flattened-mesh shadows take the depth test in place of the stencil, so that where two shadows overlap both may darken, and a shadow hides the later translucent layers behind it, which the original draws under it. | Bevy's main pass has a depth buffer without a stencil, and the depth test gives the stencil's result within one shadow. | limitation | #101 |
| Models' translucent meshes drawn twice | A mesh with translucent texels is drawn twice, its translucent copy blended in the objects' layer, back to front by the meshes' centres, where the original blends it at the model's place in the draw lists. | The models are ordered among the sprites through the depth buffer, where a blended texel cannot take the original's order; drawn opaque, the ghosts and the wings showed solid (#519). | limitation | #519 |
| Night as a pass over the frame | The frame, all but the interface, is multiplied by the night's colour in a fullscreen pass, its light per pixel from the 32 nearest light sources, where the original colours each vertex of the world. | A pass over the frame needed none of the world's materials, which were changing then (#29, #38, #41, #51); what the original blends over darkened art, the liquids, the shadows and the lamps' glows, is darkened after the blending here. | deviation | #29, #38, #41, #51 |
| Script objects' models without the offset limit | A script object's model always stands where its `0x20` point at height 0 shows, at its cell's height on its level, without the client's limit of its offset, 200 for a door or a chest and 1,000 for any other. | None of the 1,132 doors of the Seraphim's tree comes near the limit (**estimate**), and the models' frame of `0x6239C0` is not set beside the game's units. | deviation | #429, #438, #459 |
| Sectors built in the background | Walking, the sectors of the 3 × 3 about the hero are built in the background, two at once, and come in one a frame, out of view; a hero that arrives somewhere (the game's start, a teleport, a command, its rise at its home) waits in its frame for the sectors in view, those whose cells' diamonds, widened by how far what they draw reaches past them, meet the camera's view at its zoom (#1656), built at once over a pool of half the logical cores, and the rest of the 3 × 3 follows in the background. The original reads every sector of the new 3 × 3 that it has not read yet in the frame its view's centre changes, walking too, and keeps up to 96 read (docs/02 §5.1). | A sector's ground is a picture of its cells' stacks of tiles to compose and upload, some 50 to 100 ms and tens of megabytes on the overworld, against the original's inflated block of cells; the view at its widest zoom on a 16:9 screen shows less than half a sector about the hero. | limitation | #1650, #1654, #1656 |
| Soft shadows behind a setting | Besides the original's flattened shadows, the setting `[graphics] shadows = "soft"` (off by default) draws the models' shadows from a shadow map of a sun along the same lean: the models that cast a shadow cast every triangle, the ground and the models receive it at the original's third of the light left, so that a shadow follows the terrain, falls on other models and a model shades itself; the sprites flat in a known plane receive it there too, the upper storeys' floors in theirs and the underlays (the paths' stones, the flowers and the small things on the ground) on their cells' ground, and so do the effects laid on the ground in theirs (the health rings, the marks: #2605); the other sprites (trees, buildings and their ground floors' insides, fences, the fields' plants) neither cast nor receive it, so that a shadow over them is not seen, where the original's darkens them. | The maintainer's choice (#1436), from the comparison's frames: Bevy's shadow map with its Gaussian filter, 4096 texels a side, one cascade over the camera's view, about 0.6 ms a frame at 3840 × 2160 on an RTX 4080. | deviation | #1436, #2605 |
| Sprites ordered through the depth buffer | Every sprite is a quad in the screen's plane at its cell centre's depth, the underlays of lists 0 and 2 set 4,000 and 2,000 units back, the statics' shadows of list 1 3,000 back and list 4 1,000 forward, where the original draws the ground and then five lists by the cells' diagonals. | The 3D models must interleave with the sprites; a large sprite owned by one cell can still be ordered wrongly against them. | limitation | #100 |
| Sprites' opaque and translucent texels apart | A sprite is drawn twice, its opaque texels through the depth buffer and its translucent ones blended in layers in the original's order of the lists, within a layer by their meshes' centres, where the original blends every sprite in the draw lists' order. | The sprites are ordered through the depth buffer, which a blended texel cannot write; alpha to coverage, used before, dotted the sprites' edges (#35). | limitation | #35, #100, #118 |
| Textures as KTX2 with mipmaps | `build` writes the models', the items', the effects' and the loading screens' textures as KTX2 with full mip chains, BC7 where opaque and 8-bit RGBA with each level's alpha raised where not, sampled trilinearly, where the original has no mip levels. The world's tile sheets and atlases and the cursor stay 8-bit RGBA of one level, and the sprites' images are 8-bit RGBA of 4 levels, which the game packs into its pages (#839). | BC7 takes a quarter of 8-bit RGBA's memory and the mipmaps keep the models from shimmering; the maintainer's choice (#694, #700). | deviation | #694, #700, #816 |
| Trees' fade per pixel | The fade of the trees and walls about the hero is computed at every point of the sprite, around the hero's feet raised 60 pixels, where the original computes it at the corners of each piece of up to 64 × 64 pixels, around the centre of the hero's bounds. | A sprite is one quad here, and the model's bounds change with every pose; per point the fade is smooth where the original's shows the pieces' edges. | limitation |  |
| Undead's sparks from the bones | The undead goblins' red sparks as they burn (#1262) leave the corpse's bones rather than its model's skinned vertices; the dusts of the bodies' ends (781's variants 4 and 5, which take the vertices since #2514) ignore the renderer's wind. | The goblins' sparks came before the vertices were read on the CPU (`vertices`), and the rising sparks hide where each began; the wind is a **hypothesis** that no effect follows yet. | limitation | #1261 |
| Pong's turns in the world's plane | The scripts' Pong's ball (1157) turns in the world's plane on the ground, off a bat exactly along the rows and off a wall along the axis of the step that crossed into the stopping cell, where the original turns it in the flight's frame, whose axes are scaled apart, about 3° off the rows (`0x5ABD80`, `0x5A9152`). | The ball's flight and the bats' tests are the world's; a few degrees change where a ball goes but not the game. | deviation | #2008 |
| The guns' flash at the muzzle | A gun's flash (1172, `gun_fires`) keeps to its gun's muzzle, the bone `Canon_fx01`, and turns with its barrel while it shows, where the original places it once at the muzzle as the shot's clip starts and leaves it there (its FX case follows nothing). | As the shot's clip starts, the game's skeleton still holds the pose of the clip before, which the shot's blends out over 0.1 s, the flash's whole life: placed once, the flash hung where the barrel had been, off the gun (#1092's frames). | deviation | #1092 |
| The effects' lights from above | An effect's Direct3D point light (#2106, `fx_lights`) is a Bevy point light, which falls by the square of the distance alone: it stands 89 of the models' units (`1/√0.000125`) over the effect's place, so that along that place's plane it falls as the fixed function's `1 / (1 + 0.000125 d²)`, as bright as the original's, but it lights the models from above rather than from the effect's own place, and fades to nothing at the range of 200 where the original's is cut at a sixth; every light shines, where the original's device takes only its count of them by their priorities. | Bevy's lights take no attenuation of their own; at the effect's own height an inverse-square light washed the models next to it white (the Daemon's form's, #1092). | limitation | #2106, #1092 |
| The war cry's ghosts grow by the time | The Dwarf's war cry's three ghosts (1023, `war_cries`) grow by `0.013·φ` each sixtieth of a second, where the original grows them by as much each run of the system, a frame (`0x7B7F10`), so faster on a faster machine. | The game's frames come as often as the display takes them; at the original's 60 runs a second they grow alike, to about 3 times their start. | deviation | #1092 |
