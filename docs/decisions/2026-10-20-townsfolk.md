# The townsfolk's day, a step at a time

AI (`opensacred-game`), 2026-10-20 (#247, of #255).

## The original

A townsperson (docs/re/ai.md, "The townsfolk") rolls one of 26 by day: a partner, an animal, home, a house, a trader, a juggler's instrument, a child's play, or a stop; at dusk, by night and in danger it goes into the houses through their doors, or runs from the threat.

## Ours

The rolls of the animal, of home, of the partners, of the traders and of the houses, and the children's play, walk as the original's (#247, #248, #250, #251, #252); the jugglers play their instruments (#253), held by the instruments' own sockets; a visit to a trader ends at the trader rather than in a house.
A townsperson goes in and out of a house by the way through its entrance, not by the door's outer and inner points (`0x43B290`, a table of 94 types not read into the game), and the door neither opens nor closes behind it: the hero passes the doors so too.
The hero cannot shoo the children, and they say nothing: the hero's keys and the texts over the creatures are not the AI's yet (#270); they flee from the Daemon (#269), and the Vampiress is not a class of the game.
At a meeting a woman never slaps her partner (the attack's clip in one case of 11, **hypothesis** for its sense): the NPCs' graphs hold no attack's clip yet.
The evening's way (#249) is followed but for the torches, which wait for the lights in hand (#61); a townsperson takes it at the hero's looks rather than at every frame, as its day's decisions.
In danger (#254) a townsperson runs 700 from the threat, as the original's does where no door is near, rather than into the nearest house (#248); a walking one finishes its walk first, rather than turning at once; the fights' targets, which no creature has yet, put none in danger; and before the Daemon (#269) a townsperson always fears it, the hero having solved no quest, without the lines of its fear (#270).

## Why

Each of the left-out outcomes needs what the game does not have yet, the doors and their houses, the traders' tags, the instruments, the texts over the creatures, the hero's hostiles in a fight; a stop is what the original's townsperson does in most of its rolls, so the town looks as it does, if quieter, until they come.
